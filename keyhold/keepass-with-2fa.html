Keyhold

Like KeePass, with two-factor codes built in

KeePass got the basics right: your passwords sit in one encrypted file that only your master password opens, and nobody else keeps a copy. Keyhold works the same way and adds what most people now need on top of it.

The same idea

  • One vault, one master password. Several vaults can live side by side, each with its own password.
  • The file is yours: on your devices, in folders you choose and, if you turn it on, in your own Google Drive — already encrypted.
  • Free and open source, no account, no Keyhold server.

Built in

  • Two-factor codes (TOTP) with a countdown and the next code in the last seconds.
  • Logins and codes offered right under the field in Chrome, Edge and Firefox and in Android apps; new logins are offered for saving.
  • Fingerprint on the phone or Windows Hello on the computer for the entries you mark.
  • A printable recovery key that opens the vault if the master password is ever forgotten.
  • Automatic backups in seven copies, from the latest to one about a year old.
  • Phone and computer kept in step through your own Google Drive.

Bring your KeePass database

Choose Import, pick your .kdbx file (KDBX 3.1 or 4) and type its master password. Logins and groups come along, and so do two-factor codes saved by KeePassXC, KeePass 2.47 and later, or the common TOTP plugins. Your KeePass file stays as it was.
